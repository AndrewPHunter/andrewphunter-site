I'm a systems architect and builder. I design and build software systems that stay understandable and governable as they scale — and I care as much about the reasoning underneath as the build itself: why a system behaves the way it does, and what it costs when that behavior has to change.

Most of what gets written about software and AI is either prophecy or recipes — grand claims on one side, disposable how-tos on the other. I work from the register in between: someone who has built the thing and can explain why it holds. In practice that means naming boundaries, preserving constraints, and treating governance as something that improves delivery instead of slowing it.

What I write about spans architecture, delivery, AI, and transformation — where the discipline holds, where it breaks, and what it costs when it doesn't. The longer arguments run as series.

I also advise private equity firms and the companies they hold on transformation in the AI era, at **[consulting.andrewphunter.com](https://consulting.andrewphunter.com)**.
