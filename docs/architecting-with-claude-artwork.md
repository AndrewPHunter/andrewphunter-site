## Generation prompt

Use case: illustration-story / editorial article cover.
Create a finished, beautifully illustrated landscape cover for Andrew Hunter's essay "Architecting with Claude". This will be both a website social sharing card and a directly uploaded LinkedIn post image. Desired canvas 1200 by 630 pixels, 1.905:1 wide landscape composition.

Art direction: thoughtful illustrated technology magazine cover, textured night workshop, deep ink navy #0e1524, warm ivory #fafaf5, restrained muted brass #d4ad5a. Tactile paper grain and painted print texture, warm light, sophisticated editorial drawing. Match the feeling of a vintage illustrated book cover: human engineering work, not slick 3D or stock corporate imagery.

Composition: Large, exceptionally readable warm ivory serif headline in the upper left and left-center, exact text on two lines:
"Architecting"
"with Claude"
Smaller byline at lower left: "Andrew Hunter".
The title must remain clear when seen as a small thumbnail, with generous negative space behind it and all type inside a 6% safe inset. It should dominate the composition.

On the right and across the lower-right portion, a wooden architect's drafting table at an inviting three-quarter angle. A large slightly tilted drafting board holds a beautifully drawn navy system blueprint: a handful of clean ivory rectangular modules, branching paths and precise connection points, clearly a software/system plan rather than a building plan. A brass drafting lamp lights the plan. From the bottom edge of the plan, three separate slender brass paths or guide rails lead toward three small distinct terminal workstations arranged on the workbench. Their softly illuminated screens suggest separate assigned tasks with sparse code-like marks, a dependency diagram, and verification checks; these are pictorial marks, not readable paragraphs. The visual idea is one considered design coordinating several bounded streams of work. An ivory annotated sheet, pencil and ruler may lie on the table as quiet supporting details. No people needed.

Preserve a strong editorial hierarchy: headline first, blueprint second, three delegated workstations third. The right-side scene must not obscure any title letter. Warm practical light against deep navy; textured and hand-crafted, with crisp legible typography.
Text must be spelled exactly; only readable text is the headline and author byline. No subtitle, logo, watermark, robot, brain, hologram, neon cyberpunk, fake metrics, floating cloud icons, or company branding. Opaque background.
